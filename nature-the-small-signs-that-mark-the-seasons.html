The Small Signs That Mark the Seasons

Seasonal change rarely arrives all at once. It often appears through small shifts in light, temperature, growth, colour, and the behaviour of the places around us.

Notice the Light

One of the easiest seasonal changes to notice is the quality and timing of daylight. Mornings and evenings gradually feel different as the year moves forward.

Pay attention to when familiar spaces become bright or shaded. Even without checking a calendar, changes in light can provide a strong sense that the season is moving on.

Look for New Growth

Plants often provide some of the clearest signs of transition. New leaves, buds, flowers, fallen foliage, or changes in the colour of grass can all reveal what is happening around you.

You do not need to identify every plant to appreciate the change. Simply noticing what is appearing or disappearing can make regular walks more engaging.

Listen for Differences

Outdoor sound changes throughout the year. Bird activity, insects, wind through leaves, rain, and even the sounds of people using outdoor spaces can create a different atmosphere.

Taking a moment to listen can reveal seasonal details that are easy to overlook when you are focused only on what you can see.

Pay Attention to Temperature

The first noticeably cooler morning or a warmer evening can change how a familiar place feels. These shifts can influence what people wear, where they sit, and how long they stay outside.

Temperature is not always dramatic. Small differences are often enough to signal that the season is moving towards something new.

Keep Your Own Notes

If you enjoy noticing seasonal patterns, make a few simple notes when something catches your attention. A short sentence about the first flowers, a particular evening light, or a change in the trees can become an interesting record over time.

The benefit is not creating a detailed journal. It is simply becoming more aware of changes that might otherwise pass unnoticed.
