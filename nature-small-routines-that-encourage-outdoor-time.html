Small Routines That Encourage Outdoor Time

Outdoor time becomes easier to maintain when it is connected to things you already do. Small routines can create regular opportunities without requiring a major change to your schedule.

Start With One Reliable Moment

Pick a part of the day that already has a natural pause. It might be the beginning of the morning, a lunch break, or the period before dinner.

Rather than trying to change your entire routine, use that existing moment as an opportunity to step outside for a short while.

Take the Longer Way

When practical and safe, adding a little extra walking to an existing journey can be an easy way to spend more time outdoors.

The advantage is that the outdoor time is attached to something you were already going to do, so it does not require a completely separate activity.

Keep Outdoor Breaks Short

A routine does not have to take a long time. A few minutes outside can be enough to break up a day and change your surroundings.

Keeping the first version of the habit short can make it easier to repeat, especially during busy periods.

Make the Next Step Obvious

Reduce small barriers where you can. Keep a suitable jacket accessible, know which nearby route you want to use, or decide in advance where you can sit for a few minutes.

The fewer decisions you need to make, the easier it can be to follow through.

Let the Routine Change

Outdoor habits do not have to look identical every day. Some days may suit a walk, while others may only allow a few minutes outside.

Flexibility keeps a routine useful. The aim is to create more opportunities to be outdoors, not another strict task to complete.
